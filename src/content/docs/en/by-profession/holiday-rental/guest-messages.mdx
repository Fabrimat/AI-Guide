<LessonSummary>

- Give ChatGPT the facts (dates, times, rules) and the tone. Codes and passwords come from you: the draft keeps placeholders.
- To translate, ask for the translation and then the back-translation into your own language, in a new chat, to check the meaning held.
- A critical review gets a calm reply, written for future guests, with no personal details. Refunds and promises are your decision, not the AI's.
- Always reread the draft before sending: the AI tends to promise more than you would.

</LessonSummary>

This lesson starts from the instructions in [Set up ChatGPT for your B&B](/en/by-profession/holiday-rental/start-here/). If you do not have them, paste your house facts at the start of the chat.

Giulia has a German family arriving at Casa del Glicine from 12 to 15 June. She has to write the pre-arrival message, then the instructions to get in. Meanwhile a three-star review has appeared on the booking site: lovely place, but you could hear the road at night. Giulia writes in Italian; the examples here are given in English.

## The pre-arrival message

Compare a weak request with one that works.

<PromptCompare
  weak="Write a message for guests who are arriving."
  strong={`Write a welcome message for Anna and her family, who arrive at Casa del Glicine from 12 to 15 June.
Facts to use, there are no others: check-in from 3 pm to 7 pm, if they arrive later they message me. Keys are in a box next to the door, I will send the code the day before (leave [KEY BOX CODE]). Free parking in the courtyard. If they have breakfast allergies they tell me by 9 June.
Friendly tone, informal address, eight lines at most. Do not add services or rules I have not given you.`}
  note="The second one holds the facts, the placeholder for the code and the length limit. The first leaves the AI to invent times and services."
/>

You do not write the key box code in the prompt: ChatGPT leaves the placeholder and you add it in the final message. The same goes for the Wi-Fi password. For the guest, use just the first name.

## Translate and check

Giulia asks for the German version and, right after, the back-translation into Italian. Better still, she opens a new chat and pastes only the German text with the request: "Translate it into Italian, faithfully, without improving it". A chat that has not seen the original cannot translate while remembering what you meant.

The first sentence, in German:

> Liebe Anna, wir freuen uns, euch vom 12. bis 15. Juni in der Casa del Glicine zu begrüßen. Der Check-in ist von 15 bis 19 Uhr.

And the back-translation (here in English):

> Dear Anna, we are happy to welcome you from 12 to 15 June at Casa del Glicine. Check-in is from 3 to 7 pm.

Compare figures and dates first, then the tone: German `euch` is informal, and if you want the formal register ask for "Sie". If you do not read the language, the back-translation is your check, not a guarantee. For messages with important times or rules, keep sentences simple: they translate better.

## Instructions to get in

The same pattern works for the message the day before: times, key box, parking, who to write to if there is a problem. You list the facts, ask for a short text in your language and then in the languages you need. You add the code by hand, only in the final message.

## The three-star review

Giulia pastes the text of the review (it is already public, with no private data) and writes what she wants to say: thank you, the road is close, the back rooms are quieter, I offer earplugs. She does not write "offer a discount": she has not decided to.

On Airbnb the public reply is written from your profile: **Reviews**, then **Reviews about you**, then **Leave public response**, and **Submit**. It shows up right away next to the review. Verified in November 2026 on Airbnb's help page. Booking.com lets you reply from the property's dashboard, with a check before publication.

The reply is written for whoever reads it later, not to win over its author. Below is the AI's draft: find the sentences you should not send.

<SpotTheError
  prompt="Reply to the three-star review about the road noise."
  sentences={[
    { text: "Thank you for your stay and for your comments about our B&B." },
    { text: "We are sorry you could hear the road at night." },
    { text: "We will refund 20% of your stay for the inconvenience.", wrong: true, why: "You did not decide on a refund. The AI invented a financial commitment." },
    { text: "The rooms at the back are quieter, and we offer earplugs to anyone who asks." },
    { text: "We have already installed double glazing throughout the house.", wrong: true, why: "It is not among the facts you gave it. A false, public sentence that stays online." },
    { text: "We hope to see you again soon." },
  ]}
/>

Look at what happened: two sentences out of six were a promise Giulia never made. That is why the instructions say "do not decide what to offer".

<Collapsible title="A reply template to adapt">

```text
Dear guest, thank you for your stay and for your comments.
We are sorry you could hear the road at night. The rooms at the back are quieter: we are happy to offer them to anyone who asks, and we have earplugs available.
We hope to see you again soon.
Giulia, Casa del Glicine
```

</Collapsible>

No guest name beyond the first name, no dates of birth, no point-by-point argument. If the review is unfair, stay courteous anyway: others will read it.
