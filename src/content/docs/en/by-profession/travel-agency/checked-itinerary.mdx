<LessonSummary>

- Claude is good at ordering an itinerary and reminding you what to check: holidays, closing days, transfer times, time zones, season.
- It does not know strikes, current timetables, minor local holidays or extraordinary closures. You check those yourself, on official sources.
- Ask it to split the answer into two lists: what is reasonably certain and what needs verifying, with the suggested source.
- If you use web search, open the sources it cites: look at the site and the date before trusting them.

</LessonSummary>

An itinerary seems right when it reads well. The problem is that text that reads well can contain a museum closed on the wrong day. Claude is a good assistant for building and reviewing an itinerary, as long as you know what you can ask it and what you must verify yourself.

You need the project from [Set up Claude for your agency](/en/by-profession/travel-agency/start-here/). Client data stays minimal, as explained in [Company data and privacy](/en/at-work/company-data-and-privacy/). The example is in Italy, so the holidays are Italian.

## An example: Padua and Venice over All Saints' weekend

An invented couple, the Ferris, want a long weekend in the Veneto from Saturday 30 October to Tuesday 2 November 2027. You write to Claude:

```text
Build a 4-day itinerary between Padua and Venice from Saturday 30 October to Tuesday 2 November 2027, for a couple travelling by train. Interests: art and food, relaxed pace. Then check it yourself: flag holidays, days when museums and sights may be closed, tight timings, effects of the season. Split the answer into "Reasonably certain" and "To verify, and where".
```

In the answer Claude proposes the days and flags, among other things, that Monday 1 November is a national holiday (All Saints), that many museums close one day a week so each should be checked, that at the end of October the days are short and Venice can have high water. Then it lists what to verify: opening hours and closures of each place on their own sites, train times, any strikes.

It is a good base. Your work begins with the "to verify" list.

## What Claude does not know or gets wrong

Claude writes plausible text, and the information that changes often is where it is most likely to be wrong. Here it is, with where to check.

- **Transport strikes.** It does not know them. The official calendar is the [strike page of the Ministry of Infrastructure and Transport](https://scioperi.mit.gov.it/): you can search by date, sector (air, rail, local public transport) and region. Check it for the days of the trip and again a few days before.
- **Current timetables for trains, flights, ferries.** The ones it quotes are indicative. You need the carriers' sites or the official timetable for the day.
- **Minor local holidays.** Patron saints' days, village festivals, fairs. Claude remembers the big ones, not always the small ones. Check the town's site or the tourist board.
- **Extraordinary closures.** Works, exhibitions being set up, maintenance days. Only the place's official pages count.
- **Holidays abroad.** Many countries move dates every year (religious holidays, bridge days). Check the country's official site or tourist board for that year.
- **Opening hours and entrance prices.** They change with the season and with new management. The source is the museum's site.

The method is always the same: Claude tells you what to verify, you open the official source and confirm.

## How to use web search

If your plan offers it, click the **+** button at the lower left of the chat box and choose **Web search**. If you cannot find it, it is a limit of the plan or the interface, and you can still work with the checklist.

With search on, Claude searches the web and cites sources. Check like this:

1. Open the links it cites, do not stop at the summary.
2. Look at the site: official (museum, town, carrier, ministry) or a travel blog?
3. Look at the date of the page: a 2022 timetable does not hold for 2027.
4. If two sources disagree, the official one wins.

Verified in November 2026. Web search counts toward your plan's usage limit, and the interface may change.

## A routine in four moves

1. Ask for the itinerary with real dates (year included) and number of people.
2. Ask for the two lists: reasonably certain, to verify.
3. You check the items on the list against official sites and the strike calendar.
4. Go back to Claude with the corrections ("the museum closes on Monday, move the visit to Tuesday") and redo the itinerary.
