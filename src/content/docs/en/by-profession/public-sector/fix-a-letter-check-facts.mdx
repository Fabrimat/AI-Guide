<LessonSummary>

- To revise a letter, ask Copilot for two separate passes: first the revision (clarity, tone, errors), then the list of facts to check.
- Ask it to list every change it made: that way you see whether it changed the meaning.
- AI invents articles, paragraphs and law numbers with the same confidence it writes everything else. Every legal reference is looked up in an official source before it stays in the letter.
- If you can't find it, remove it. Don't correct it from memory.
- Deadlines, amounts, hours and office names come from the agency, not from Copilot.

</LessonSummary>

:::note
This lesson is about Italian public administration: the sources named here (Normattiva, Gazzetta Ufficiale) are Italian, and the letter in the example would be in Italian. The office is invented.
:::

Dalla Torre, of the Technical Office of the Comune di Valdoria (invented), wrote in a hurry a reply to Ms. Marchetti, who asked to put an outdoor seating area in front of her cafe. The letter is too long, a bit curt, and full of awkward sentences. Before sending it, he asks Copilot to fix it. As in [Replying to citizens](/en/by-profession/public-sector/citizen-replies/), he first removes her personal data.

## Step one: the revision

Dalla Torre pastes the letter and writes:

```text
Revise this letter to a citizen. Goals: make it clearer, more courteous and free of language errors.
Rules: do not change the facts, do not add information, do not insert references to laws or regulations that are not already in the text.
At the end give me two lists: the changes you made (one line each) and everything in the letter that is a fact to check (dates, deadlines, laws, office names).
```

The request has two parts. The list of changes shows whether Copilot altered the meaning of a sentence to make it prettier. The list of facts is for step two.

## Step two: the check

The revised letter arrives like this. Try to find the wrong sentences before reading the solution.

The method is simple. For each item on the list of facts, look at where it came from before Copilot: the original letter (fine, recheck anyway), your agency's regulation (open the regulation), nowhere (remove it or ask the officer in charge).

## Where to check laws

For national laws there are two official sources.

- **[Normattiva](https://www.normattiva.it)** has the up-to-date texts of laws. Search by law number and year, open the whole act, read the article you need. The "in force" text can differ from the original, because laws get amended.
- **[Gazzetta Ufficiale](https://www.gazzettaufficiale.it)** publishes the original text as it came out.

For municipal regulations the source is the agency's website (usually the "Amministrazione trasparente" section or the regulations collection) or the office that keeps them. For EU law there is [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu).

An example of a correct check: Law no. 132 of 23 September 2025 exists and is on the [Gazzetta Ufficiale site](https://www.gazzettaufficiale.it/eli/id/2025/09/25/25G00143/sg). If Copilot cites "art. 14" of that law, open the act and read the paragraph, because the article number may be right and the content it attributes to it wrong. Everything counts: the law number, the year, the article, the paragraph and what the paragraph says.

If you can't reach the law at that moment, write the letter without the legal reference ("under the municipal regulation") and add the citation once you've found it. A citation added later costs five minutes. A wrong citation sent to a citizen costs much more.

## Copilot doesn't replace whoever signs

Article 14, paragraph 2, of law 132/2025 says the person remains solely responsible for the measures and procedures in which AI was used. For a letter, that means Dalla Torre rereads it, checks the citations and signs it, even if Copilot wrote it. If your agency requires flagging the use of AI in documents, follow that rule: the law sets out the principle of traceability, the concrete way is set by the agency.
