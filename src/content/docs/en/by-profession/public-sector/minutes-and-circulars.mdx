<LessonSummary>

- Copilot puts scattered notes into your office's format: present and absent, topics, decisions, actions.
- Give it only your notes and the template. If a piece of data is missing, it must write [TO COMPLETE], not invent it.
- The check that matters: is there anything in the minutes that nobody said? Reread with your notes next to you.
- Keep decisions and discussion apart. Something proposed is not something decided.
- From decision to internal circular is a short step, but the resulting text is yours.

</LessonSummary>

:::note
This lesson is about Italian public administration. The office in the example is invented; notes and minutes would be in Italian, the examples here are translated.
:::

The Technical Office of the Comune di Valdoria (invented) meets on Wednesdays to coordinate road maintenance. Giorgio, the meeting's secretary, takes notes by hand and then turns them into minutes in an hour. Today he wants to do it with Copilot. First he reads [What you can do with Copilot, and what you can't](/en/by-profession/public-sector/start-here/): the meeting is about works, not citizens, so his notes contain no personal data. If yours do, anonymize them as in [Replying to citizens](/en/by-profession/public-sector/citizen-replies/).

## From notes to minutes

Giorgio's notes look like everyone's: fragments, abbreviations, arrows.

```text
Wednesday 4 Nov, 10 am, meeting room.
Present: Bruni (office head), Costa, Dalla Torre, Esposito. Absent: Fabbri.
Paving via Marconi: Costa says the contractor can start on 17 Nov. Bruni wants to hear the local police first about the closure. Decided to contact them by Friday.
Potholes via Roma: discussed, no decision. Dalla Torre brings the quotes next time.
To do: Costa writes to the contractor, Dalla Torre quotes.
Next: Wednesday 11 Nov, 10 am.
```

With the office format at hand, Giorgio writes to Copilot:

```text
Turn these notes into meeting minutes. Use this structure: date and place, present, absent, agenda, for each item a "Discussion" and a "Decision" section (if there is none, write "No decision"), actions with who and by when, date of the next meeting.
Use only what is in the notes. Where information is missing write [TO COMPLETE]. Do not add names, dates, reasons or sentences said by anyone that are not in the notes.
Dry style, past tense.

[notes]
```

The resulting minutes have the sections in the right place. Look at what Copilot does where the notes are vague: for via Marconi it writes "Decision: contact the local police by Friday" and "[TO COMPLETE]" as the deadline for answering the contractor, because Giorgio hadn't written one. Had you not forbidden it, it might have picked a date.

## Checking that nothing was added

Minutes have value because they record what happened. An assistant that summarizes tends to make sentences sharper than they were said. Giorgio does three checks, with his notes next to him.

1. **Present and absent.** Are the names right? Is Fabbri marked absent?
2. **Decisions and discussion.** Does every "it was decided" match a decision taken in the meeting? The via Roma potholes are discussion, not decision.
3. **Dates, amounts, names.** Is every number and every date in the notes? If a date appears only in the minutes, it is invented.

## If the meeting is on Teams

If the meeting is on Teams, Copilot can use the transcript instead of notes. Several things must be in place together and you don't decide them: transcription must be on, the organizer must have allowed recording or transcription, and the intelligent recap needs a Microsoft Copilot or Teams Premium licence. Ask the IT office what is active in your agency. Participants receive a notice when transcription starts. If the meeting discusses people, the transcript contains them, so your agency's rule on personal data applies.

A transcript reads worse than notes (broken sentences, wrong names). The check on the minutes stays the same, with more comparing to do.

## From decision to circular

The via Marconi decision also concerns other offices: the local police must be told, the front office must know what to say to citizens. Giorgio writes to Copilot:

```text
Write an 8-line internal circular to all services. Fact: the via Marconi paving works start on 17 November, the road will be closed to traffic and the local police have been notified. What the services must do: send their access requests by [DATE]. Clear and courteous tone, plain language, no bureaucratic phrasing. Use only these facts.
```

Copilot proposes a draft with a subject line, one line on what happens, one on what to do and one on who to contact. Giorgio fills in [DATE] with the head of the office and sends it for approval. Bruni signs the circular.
