You opened an electricity bill and got stuck on the line "system
charges adjustment factor". You read a contract and didn't
understand what "automatic renewal unless cancelled within thirty
days of expiry" actually means. Or you got a medical report with
acronyms that look like military codes. It's happened to everyone.

Your attention isn't the issue: these documents are written to
protect the people who send them, not to make sense to you. A
conversational AI can take a difficult text and give it back to you
in normal language. It does the same job on contracts, condo bylaws,
tax authority letters, drug leaflets, insurance policy terms. It's
one of the most practical uses in everyday life.

In this lesson we'll see three techniques that work almost every time, with a real example on an electricity bill. About medical reports, a heads-up: the AI is a good translator of acronyms and clinical terms, not a substitute for a doctor in choosing what to do. We cover it in [*What not to do*](/en/everyday-use/what-not-to-do/#1-health), the last lesson of the module.

## The three techniques

You don't need a magic prompt, just a clear idea of what you're
asking for.

### Explain it like I'm ten

The base technique. You paste (or describe) the difficult text and ask the AI to explain it in simple words: a clause, a page of a regulation, a paragraph of an article. You decide the level: "like I'm ten" is the extreme case, but you can also ask *"like to an adult who knows nothing about the topic"* or *"like to a colleague who knows half of what I know"*. The more precise you are about your starting point, the better the explanation fits.

> *"Explain this contract sentence as if I knew nothing about law:
> 'In case of early termination, the customer is required to pay the
> fee provided in Annex B, paragraph 3, plus administrative
> charges.'"*

You'll get an answer like: "if you close the contract before the end
date you pay a penalty, which is written in another sheet of the
contract, Annex B, plus a small handling fee". Same meaning,
different language.

### Extract just what I'm interested in

The difference from the first technique is the focus: there you want
to *understand* the document, here you only want a *piece of data*.
Often you don't need the whole thing explained: you just need a
direct answer to a specific question. *When do I have to pay? How
much does it cost to cancel? When does the coverage start?* The AI
pulls the data out of the text without making you read twenty pages.

> *"From this bill, tell me only: total amount, due date, and how
> much the fixed monthly fee is."*

The model reads the whole document but only answers what you asked. It's the right pattern for long documents (an insurance policy, a condo bylaws document, an information sheet) when you need a specific piece of information and nothing more.

### Tell me what to watch for

The third technique is the most useful on contracts, before you sign them. Instead of asking "explain it" or "extract a piece of data", ask the AI to do the job of a knowledgeable friend: *flag the things that could give me trouble*.

> *"Read this rental contract and tell me which clauses could be
> disadvantageous for me as a tenant. Focus on penalties, automatic
> renewals, minimum duration, and expenses that might come back to
> me."*

The AI doesn't sign anything for you, but it pulls out a list of the
clauses to slow down on: automatic renewal, non-refundable deposit,
binding minimum duration, penalties hidden in the footnotes. On a
rental, a typical answer sounds like: *"24-month minimum term with a
240-euro penalty if you leave early; automatic renewal unless you
cancel three months before expiry; extraordinary maintenance is on
the tenant, this is a clause to verify against local law."* It's useful before signing a phone offer, an electricity contract, a gym membership, an insurance policy, a preliminary contract: it gives you a second pair of eyes before a decision that locks you in.

:::tip[What if my document is on paper or a long PDF?]
Selectable text (web page, email) you copy and paste. For a PDF almost all AIs accept the file uploaded directly, as you'll see in *Photos, images, and files*. A paper document you type out (a bill takes five minutes) or photograph. For very long documents, combine these techniques with those of *Summarize a long document*.
:::

:::note[Before pasting in a document of yours (or someone else's)]
What you paste reaches the provider's servers. A bill has your address and your POD (the meter code), a contract may have a tax ID and IBAN, a medical report has health data. Delete or replace with `xxx` anything the question doesn't need: you need the amounts, you don't need your name. If the document belongs to someone else, ask their OK first and anonymize their data like your own. The full picture is in [*What you share when you use AI*](/en/fundamentals/privacy-basics/).
:::

## An example: the electricity bill

The first two techniques at work on a real scene: a bill that came
in higher than usual, and you want to understand why.

<ChatDemo
  user={`Explain this electricity bill. Total amount 142.30 euros for November. The line items are:
- Energy commodity charge: 58.40
- Network and metering fee: 27.80
- System charges: 19.50
- Taxes (excise + VAT): 22.10
- Adjustment from previous billing: 14.50

What exactly am I paying for, and why is there that adjustment?`}
  ai={`I'll go item by item in plain language.

"Energy commodity charge" (€58.40) is what you actually pay for the kilowatt-hours you used. The real "cost of electricity".

"Network and metering fee" (€27.80) is what you pay the grid operator to bring electricity to your home, plus the meter reading.

"System charges" (€19.50) are costs the State puts on every bill to fund things like incentives for renewables and social subsidies.

"Taxes" (€22.10) are excise on energy plus VAT.

"Adjustment from previous billing" (€14.50) is the line you're asking about. In an earlier bill the supplier had estimated your consumption, and now they're correcting it because the actual figure (read from the meter) was higher. It's a back-charge, not a new cost: you're paying for energy you'd already used.

To give you the order of magnitude: actual consumption is about 41% of the total. The rest is infrastructure, taxes, and a retroactive correction.`}
/>

In four lines of prompt you got a complete translation and found the culprit of the higher amount: a retroactive adjustment, not runaway consumption. With *"why is it high?"* (technique 2) you'd have had a direct answer, with *"tell me if anything looks off"* (technique 3) a sweep of the whole bill.

## What to always verify

**The AI often gets numbers wrong.** It mixes up line items, adds
badly, swaps decimals and thousands, takes a value from one row and
assigns it to another. It happens even on trivial sums: it'll give
you a total of 141.80 instead of 142.30 because it read 27.30
instead of 27.80, and you won't catch it unless you double-check.
It happens because it isn't computing on a calculator: it's
predicting plausible text, and a plausible number isn't a correct
number. You saw the mechanism in
[*When to trust it (and when
not)*](/en/fundamentals/when-to-trust-it/).

Numbers are the obvious case, but the AI can be wrong on words too: a clause translated too softly, an exception skipped, a detail added that isn't in the text. The counter-move is to ask for the exact sentence it drew the explanation from (*"which line of the contract did you get this from?"*). If it can't point to it, or cites one that doesn't exist, you've found the weak spot.

:::caution[Paste, ask, verify]
Use the AI to *understand* the document, not to trust the numbers it gives you back. If it tells you the penalty is €200, go read the exact figure in the original text. Translating is its job, checking the figures stays yours: it applies to amounts, dates, percentages, tax IDs and phone numbers.
:::

Try it. You asked the AI to flag what to look at in a rental contract, and this is its answer. It contains two subtle errors: find them by comparing with the contract.

<SpotTheError
  prompt="Rental contract: 24-month term, 240-euro penalty if you leave early, automatic renewal unless cancelled at least three months before expiry, non-refundable deposit of two months' rent. Tell me what to look at."

    { text: "The minimum term is 24 months." },
    { text: "If you leave before expiry you pay a 240-euro penalty." },
    { text: "If you don't cancel, the contract renews automatically for another 24 months.", wrong: true, why: "The text mentions automatic renewal but not for how long: the AI added a detail that isn't in the contract." },
    { text: "To avoid renewal you must send notice at least three months before expiry." },
    { text: "The two-month deposit is returned to you at the end of the contract.", wrong: true, why: "The contract defines it as non-refundable: the AI wrote the opposite." },
  ]}
/>
