You wrote a clear request, you pulled all the levers from the previous
lesson, you hit enter. The answer is "ok": not wrong, but not exactly
what you needed. What do you do?

The most common reflex is to close the chat and try again with a new
prompt, hoping to hit it right on the second try. It's the wrong move.
People who use AI well do the opposite: **they keep the conversation
going**. An "ok" answer that becomes good with two lines of follow-up
is the normal pattern, not the exception.

## The iteration cycle

Using AI well looks like a dialogue, not a command. You write the
request, you read the answer, you say what to adjust, you get the
better version. Repeat until you're there.

Three types of follow-up cover ninety percent of the cases.

### Add a constraint

Is the first answer too generic? Add a piece of context that narrows
the field. *"Budget max 50 euros"*. *"No dairy"*. *"For someone
who's never programmed"*. You don't rewrite the original request: you
only add the new constraint.

### Change the angle

The answer is in the wrong shape, not in the wrong content. *"Too
long, give it to me in three bullets"*. *"Too technical, explain it
like I'm ten"*. *"Skip the history, just the practical consequences
today"*. The role, the tone, the length: they change with one
sentence.

### Deepen a piece

The overall answer is fine, but one part interests you more.
*"Explain point 2 better"*. *"Ok on the overall plan, but give me an
hour-by-hour schedule for Saturday"*. *"Can you cite the two or
three main sources?"*. The AI doesn't repeat everything: it develops
only where you pointed the finger.

You can combine two of them in a single message (*"make it shorter
**and** add the 50-euro budget"*) if the case is simple, or keep them
separate across different turns if you want to see the effect of
each constraint before adding the next.

One important thing: **the AI remembers the whole chat**. You don't
have to rewrite the initial context every time. If you told it earlier
that you're planning a weekend in Florence with a 200-euro budget, it
keeps that in mind for all the later messages in the same
conversation. You saw this in
[*How does the AI "understand"?*](/en/fundamentals/how-ai-understands/):
there's memory inside the same chat, none outside.

:::note[If the chat gets very long]
Chat memory still has a ceiling. In conversations that grow to
dozens or hundreds of messages, the AI may start to lose pieces from
the earlier turns. You usually notice when an answer ignores a
constraint you gave at the beginning. When that happens, remind it
in one line, or open a new chat.
:::

A new chat makes sense in two cases: when you're actually switching
topic (I stop planning the weekend, I start writing an email), and
when the current chat has gotten stuck, the AI keeps making the same
mistake, and two follow-ups aren't enough. For everything else, stay
on the same chat.

## An example: planning a weekend

The clearest way to see iteration in action is to watch it on a real
case. Planning a weekend away is perfect: it starts generic and
becomes yours through several turns.

### Turn 1: the starting request

<ChatDemo
  user="Plan me a two-day weekend in Florence."
  ai={`Here's a classic weekend in Florence.

Saturday: tour of the historic center (Duomo, Baptistery, Piazza della Signoria), lunch break at a typical trattoria, afternoon at the Uffizi, dinner in the Santa Croce area.

Sunday: morning at Palazzo Pitti and the Boboli Gardens, lunch in San Niccolò, afternoon at Piazzale Michelangelo for sunset over the city.

Let me know if you want restaurant suggestions or prefer a different angle.`}
/>

"Ok" answer, generic tourist guide. I start from here.

### Turn 2: I add constraints

<ChatDemo
  user={`We're two people, we arrive Friday evening by train, no car. Total budget 200 euros each for the weekend, accommodation included. We don't like touristy restaurants.`}
  ai={`Let me revise factoring in the budget and the train.

Sleeping: a B&B in Oltrarno or San Frediano, roughly 60-80 euros per night for a double. Walking distance from the station, more livable and cheaper than the center.

Saturday: walking tour of the center in the morning (everything free from outside). Quick lunch at a wine bar in the center or at a non-touristy trattoria, 10-15 euros each. Afternoon at the Uffizi, 25 euros each if you book online. Dinner in the Sant'Ambrogio area, where Florentines go more than tourists, 25-35 euros each.

Sunday: Boboli Gardens in the morning, 10 euros. Quick lunch in San Niccolò. Afternoon at Piazzale Michelangelo, walking up (free).

Rough total: 180-200 euros each, in line with the budget.`}
/>

Now the plan has my constraints. I didn't rewrite the request from
scratch: I only added what wasn't there before.

### Turn 3: I dig deeper

<ChatDemo
  user="Great on the broad strokes. Let's do a detailed schedule for Saturday morning, we'll leave the B&B around 9."
  ai={`Here's a realistic schedule for Saturday morning, starting from Oltrarno at 9.

09:00 - breakfast at the bar downstairs (2-3 euros each).
09:30 - walk across Ponte Vecchio.
09:45 - arrive in Piazza della Signoria, outside tour of Palazzo Vecchio and the Loggia dei Lanzi.
10:30 - Duomo: church free, cupola 20 euros if booked in advance.
11:30 - Baptistery and Giotto's Bell Tower from outside, photos.
12:00 - walk toward the Mercato Centrale.
12:30 - street food lunch on the first floor of the Mercato Centrale (10-12 euros each).

Does this pace work for you, or would you prefer something slower?`}
/>

### Turn 4: changing the angle

<ChatDemo
  user="I think we packed too much into the morning. Give it to me in a more relaxed version, three stops instead of six, with time to pause."
  ai={`Slower version, same area.

09:30 - breakfast at the bar and a walk across Ponte Vecchio.
10:45 - Piazza della Signoria, outside tour, coffee sitting at the bar under the arcade.
12:15 - Mercato Centrale at your pace, street food lunch on the first floor.

This way you have time to stop and take things in, without running from one spot to the next.`}
/>

Same information as before, different shape: same places, fewer
stops, more room to breathe. It's the third type of follow-up,
"change the angle": I don't touch the content, I change how it
reaches me.

The conversation now has a tailored answer. None of these four
messages, by itself, would have been enough. Together, yes.

## Common mistakes

Three things that slow you down a lot while you're learning to
iterate.

**Starting over instead of continuing.** When an answer doesn't work,
the reflex is to close the chat and rewrite a "perfect" prompt. Often
it's slower. Adding one line to the existing chat is almost always
faster and more precise: the AI keeps the work done so far.

**Piling everything into a single request.** The opposite temptation:
putting thirty constraints into the first message to avoid later
turns. It doesn't work well. The AI loses pieces, forgets details,
gets confused. A practical rule: if rereading the request there are
more than two "and also", you've probably loaded too much. Better to
start wide and narrow down with follow-ups: the mistake shows up
sooner, and it's easier to fix.

**Forgetting that the chat has memory.** Repeating with every message
*"as I was saying"* or *"remember there are two of us"* is wasted
effort. Inside the same conversation the AI has everything in front
of it. You only have to give context again when you open a new chat.
